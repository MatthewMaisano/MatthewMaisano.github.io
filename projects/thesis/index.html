Homogeneous black holes in 5D.

My Master's thesis in theoretical physics — black holes with exotic horizon geometries in five-dimensional gauged supergravity.

Read the full thesis (PDF)

Homogeneous Black Holes in 5D Gauged Supergravity — Master’s Degree in Theoretical Physics, University of Milano-Bicocca, November 2026. Supervisors: Prof. Silke Klemm (external) and Prof. Silvia Penati (internal).

The question. In our four-dimensional world, Hawking proved that the horizon of a black hole must be a sphere. That theorem relies on a positive (or zero) cosmological constant. In anti-de Sitter (AdS) space — the negatively curved universe at the heart of the AdS/CFT correspondence, which links gravity to quantum field theories — the rules are much looser: horizons can be flat, hyperbolic, or have far stranger shapes. In five dimensions the horizon is a three-dimensional space, and Thurston’s classification gives eight possible “model geometries” for it. The familiar ones (sphere, flat, hyperbolic) are well studied; my thesis focuses on the more exotic twisted ones, Nil and Sol, and asks: can a black hole have such a horizon, and what does the spacetime around it look like?

I studied four families of black holes in five-dimensional gauged supergravity, combining analytic techniques for the first three and a numerical approach for the fourth.

1. Zooming into AdS black holes: plane waves

I applied a scaling limit (the Inönü–Wigner contraction) to the Schwarzschild–AdS5 and Reissner–Nordström–AdS5 black holes: it zooms onto one point of the horizon while boosting it to the speed of light, turning the spherical (SU(2)) symmetry into the Nil one.

Result. The limit is still an exact solution of the field equations, describing a gravitational wave with flat wave fronts (with an electromagnetic wave travelling along it in the charged case). Because of the cosmological constant it is not a standard pp-wave but a plane-fronted wave of Kundt type, the AdS analogue of the Siklos waves.

2. A black hole in a Gödel universe: time travel everywhere

The Gödel universe is a rotating spacetime famous for allowing closed timelike curves — paths that return to their own past. I studied light rays around a black hole placed in it, and then applied the same contraction.

Light rays near the Gödel black hole: they reach the "velocity-of-light surface" (dotted), stop, turn back and spiral towards the horizon.

Result. After the contraction the horizon disappears and the spacetime is no longer a black hole. Once the Nil directions are made compact, a closed timelike curve passes through every point, whatever the mass of the black hole and the size of the compact directions.

3. The magnetic Sol black hole: a numerical map

The core of the thesis is a black hole whose horizon has Sol geometry, supported by a magnetic field of strength \(p\). No analytic solution with all metric functions varying was known, and none of the ansätze I tried worked, so I built the solution numerically: a series expansion near the horizon, then a Runge–Kutta integration outwards, using one of the Einstein equations as a check on the error (~10−9).

How the geometry changes with the magnetic parameter p. Below a critical value the solutions settle to anti-de Sitter at large distance (right panel, lower dashed line); above it they break down at a finite radius.

Result: three regimes. Scanning the parameters, and locating the boundaries with a bisection algorithm, I found that everything is controlled by a critical field \(p_* = \alpha_0\sqrt{-\Lambda}\), which does not depend on the temperature of the black hole:

  • \(p < p_*\) — a regular black hole that is asymptotically AdS5, with boundary \(\mathbb{R}_t \times \mathrm{Sol}\): a new family generalising the known solutions.
  • \(p = p_*\) — exactly the analytic solution already known in the literature, recovered numerically.
  • \(p > p_*\) — two of the Sol directions collapse at a finite radius, forming a curvature singularity with a precise power-law structure (exponent 2/3), which I derived analytically and checked against the numerics.
  • In part of parameter space a second threshold \(p_\text{low}\) appears, below which the third direction collapses instead — so regular solutions exist only for \(p_\text{low} < p < p_*\).
Left: map of the solutions — green are regular, asymptotically AdS black holes; red and blue are the two ways the geometry collapses. Right: near the singularity the numerical curves become straight lines with the predicted slope 2/3.

The extremal (zero-temperature) case. Here the geometry near the horizon is AdS2 × Sol, and small deformations of it grow with an irrational power of the distance, \(k_+ = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(-1+\sqrt{35/3}\right) \simeq 1.2078\). This means the solution cannot be written as an ordinary Taylor series: I built the correct double series and matched it numerically with the Fefferman–Graham expansion at infinity, obtaining the horizon size as a function of the magnetic field.

Left: scan of 441 extremal solutions — only the teal ones (61) reach anti-de Sitter space at infinity. Right: the resulting relation between horizon size and magnetic field.

What’s next. An analytic solution guided by this numerical map (e.g. a perturbative construction around \(p_*\)), the thermodynamics of the new Sol black holes and their holographic interpretation, and a classification of the singular behaviours.

Tools. General relativity and supergravity, Lie groups and homogeneous spaces, asymptotic/series expansions, and Python for the numerics (Runge–Kutta integration, bisection, parameter scans).
